# Archival Street Documentary

**Use it for:** the history of a city, a street, a district or an event that happened in one
place — "Paris in 1889", "the day the subway opened", "London before the Blitz", "how this
market town looked a century ago". Any historical place, any era with streets.

**Do not use it for:** subjects with no place (use a quote or explainer entry), modern product
stories, or long chains of dated milestones (use timeline-eras).

## How it looks
A period film. Each narration scene is either its scene image (graded silver, sepia,
cyanotype or amber, pulled into focus like a projector and pushed or panned slowly) or, with no
image, an illustrated street: the camera glides up a one-point-perspective street between
facades with windows and awnings, people on the pavements, lamp posts or trees passing by,
and a landmark (clock tower, dome or arch) at the end, all fading into period haze. A paper
archive slip drops in at the top of each scene: "PLATE 02 · BOSTON", the date typed letter by
letter, a short label and a rubber postmark. The opening title card shows the place, the
title rising word by word and the first date. Film strip perforations (or a mounted-print
border), gate weave, flicker, scratches, dust and grain sell the archive. Scenes alternate an
iris and a dissolve, with a light leak at every cut. Karaoke serif captions at the bottom.

## Filling the props
- `title`: 2–6 words ("America's First Subway"). `place`: the city or street ("Boston").
- `plates`: one `{date, label}` per narration scene, in order. `date` is what you can verify:
  a full date, a year, a decade ("1890s") or "c. 1900" when unsure; "Today" for a present-day
  closing scene. `label` ≤ 8 words: where we are or what happens.
- The first plate's date also appears on the title card; scene 0's slip only shows if the
  first scene is longer than the title card (about 3.4 s).
- `grade`: `silver` (black and white) suits 1880–1940 photography, `sepia` the 19th century,
  `cyanotype` blueprints and early photos, `amber` tinted silent film.
- `street`: what scenes without an image show — `tramway` (rails, wires, streetcar, clock
  tower), `boulevard` (wide, trees, domed building), `lane` (narrow, signs, lanterns, arch).
- `frame`: `film` (sprocket strip), `print` (mounted photo border) or `none`.
- Write narration with facts you can check; one scene per date.

Renders `<AdWeave>`: end-card, mid-roll-card or sponsor-mention suit history channels and

## In a sequence
Themeable: with a shared look the haze, facades, slips and film strip take the look's palette
(ink streets on paper for light looks) while scene photos keep the period grade. Slips sit at
the top, clear of the shared captions. Good as the hook or context beat of a history video.

## Forking ideas
Add a horse-drawn cart lane, a night variant with lit windows, or a map inset that pins the
place on each slip.
