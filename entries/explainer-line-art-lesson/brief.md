# Line-Art Geography Lesson

**Use it for:** teaching one geography idea step by step like a classroom whiteboard lesson:
pressure belts and global winds, ocean currents, climate zones, rain shadows, sea and land
breezes, monsoons, the layers of the atmosphere or of the Earth, soil horizons, or a
comparison of regions and climate types. Works as a whole short lesson or as the "how it
works" segment of a longer geography, nature or science video.

**Do not use it for:** precise maps of countries or routes (the globe has no coastlines and
the cross-section is a generic landscape), or for data-heavy charts. Everything named must
be real and checkable; when unsure, use broader wording ("near 30°N", "about 12 km").

## How it looks
A cream paper page with ink line art (or blueprint / chalkboard). A chapter header sits top
left: step number, heading and course label over a ruled line, with step dots on the right.
Each step draws one diagram with a pen-stroke reveal, synced to the narration:

- `globe`: a sketched Earth seen from the equator, meridians slowly spinning, dashed equator,
  N/S marks. Marks with `arrow: "none"` draw coloured latitude lines with numbered badges and
  labels on the right (belts, zones, the tropics). Marks with an arrow draw a row of three
  flowing wind or current arrows at that latitude, labelled on the left.
- `section`: a side view with sea on the left and land with a mountain right of centre.
  Each mark is a flowing arrow (up = rising, down = sinking, east/west = sideways) at its
  position, with its label in a row along the top.
- `layers`: curved strata stacked top to bottom, each filled and outlined as it is
  mentioned, labels on the right.
- `panels`: 2–4 sketched cards with a drawn line icon, label, small arrow and handwritten
  detail (columns in landscape, rows in portrait).

Each mark appears when the narration first says a word of its label (or evenly spaced if
it never does), so the drawing keeps pace with the voice. An optional handwritten note with
a drawn arrow lands under the diagram. Steps change with a page-slide wipe; the camera
pushes in slowly while each step is on screen. Subtitles sit in a paper plate at the bottom.

## Timing
- One narration line per step. With one more line than steps, the first line becomes a
  title card (title, hand underline, subtitle and a sketched spinning globe).
- With fewer lines than steps, the steps share the time evenly. Content finishes before the
  end card.

## Filling the props
- `chapter`: short course label, e.g. "Geography · Climate" (optional).
- `title`: the lesson title, ≤ 52 chars. `subtitle`: optional one-liner or second-language
  title. Wrap one word in `*asterisks*` to colour it.
- `steps`: 1–8 items, one per narration line, each with:
  - `heading`: ≤ 40 chars, e.g. "Global *wind* belts".
  - `diagram`: `globe`, `section`, `layers` or `panels` (see above).
  - `marks`: 1–6 items in the order the narration mentions them:
    - `label` ≤ 28 chars; reuse the narration's words so the reveal syncs.
    - `detail` ≤ 60 chars, optional small handwritten line.
    - `at`: globe → latitude −90…90 (north positive); section → 0 (left) … 100 (right),
      keep marks ≥ 20 apart; sea is 0–28, the mountain peak is near 64; layers → relative
      thickness (1–5, not to scale); panels → leave out.
    - `tone`: `warm` (heat, low pressure, rising air), `cool` (cold, high pressure, water),
      `green` (land, plants, winds) or `ink`.
    - `arrow`: `none`, `up`, `down`, `east`, `west`. On the globe, east/west means the
      direction the wind or current flows toward (trade winds flow west, westerlies east).
    - `icon` (panels only): sun, cloud, rain, drop, leaf, mountain, wave, globe, map, pin,
      fire, house, building, people…
  - `note`: optional ≤ 80-char takeaway, written in the hand font.
- `palette`: `paper` (default), `blueprint` or `chalk`.
- `showCaptions`: subtitle plate on or off.
